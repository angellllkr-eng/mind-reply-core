# NOVA Legal & Regulatory Evidence Register

Status: CONTROLLED REGISTER — NOT LEGAL ADVICE

## Jurisdiction model

NOVA treats gambling regulation as jurisdiction-specific. There is no single EU-wide gambling approval that should be treated as a universal production permission. The European Commission notes that Member States retain significant responsibility and national enforcement roles in gambling-related matters.

Great Britain is separately controlled. The UK Gambling Commission states that providing online casino facilities to consumers in Great Britain requires the relevant remote casino operating licence. Remote licensees also operate under technical standards and testing requirements.

## Evidence classes

LEGAL_ENTITY — verified operating entity and ownership structure.
LICENCE — regulator-issued licence valid for the exact activity and market.
SUPPLIER — signed supplier agreement plus relevant supplier approvals.
PAYMENT — written payment-provider approval for the exact business/model/markets.
KYC_AML — implemented and tested controls with provider evidence.
RESPONSIBLE_GAMBLING — approved policy, tooling, monitoring and intervention process.
PLAYER_FUNDS — documented segregation/ledger/reconciliation controls where required.
TECHNICAL_TESTING — applicable game/RNG/system testing and certificates.
SECURITY — required independent assurance and current remediation evidence.
LEGAL_TEXT — counsel-approved terms, privacy, cookies, responsible-gambling and jurisdictional notices.
MARKET_ACCESS — geo controls, restrictions and market-specific approval.
DEPLOYMENT — DNS/TLS, environment, rollback and monitoring evidence.

## Claim rule

A public statement may only claim what the register can support. A provider's licence is not NOVA's licence. A design feature is not a compliance control until its implementation and operation are evidenced.

## Great Britain

Before any real-money service is offered to GB consumers, the exact NOVA operator/licensing structure must be verified against the current UK Gambling Commission requirements. ## EU

NOVA must maintain a country-by-country matrix for every intended market, including licence/notification status, permitted products, responsible authority, age requirements, advertising rules, payment constraints, localization requirements, responsible-gambling requirements and data/consumer obligations. National rules must be checked at launch and whenever a material legal change occurs.
